## Course Description

This course is an introduction to the methods of social science research as applied to criminal justice planning and evaluation. Students learn to critically evaluate published research and design and conduct research of their own. Topics covered include the ethics of research, the relationship between theory and research, variables and measurement, causality, qualitative and quantitative methods, and writing up research findings.

As an asynchronous online course, all lectures, readings, and assignments are accessed through Canvas on the student's own schedule each week.



## Course Learning Objectives

By the end of this course, students will be able to:

1. Develop a well-formed research question
2. Integrate existing literature and develop new hypotheses
3. Design a data collection strategy with measures that accurately reflect the study design
4. Analyze and communicate research findings

Broader goals include introducing students to the logic of social science research, improving their ability to assess published research, and familiarizing them with major methods for developing and answering social science questions.

## Assignments

### Course Preparation Module (50 pts)
Completed during Week 1. Includes verifying the email policy, setting up Google Doc submissions, installing Zotero, updating your course profile, and posting a video introduction on the class discussion board.

### Course Engagement (50 pts)
Consistent, active engagement with course content throughout the semester. In this asynchronous format, engagement is assessed through regular Canvas logins and access to weekly course materials.

### CITI Certification (15 pts)
Completion of CITI training for SDSU's Institutional Review Board (IRB), required for conducting research with human subjects.

### Literature Review (15 pts)
A literature review on the student's topic of interest, synthesizing existing research to situate and motivate their research question.

### RQ Presentation (20 pts)
A presentation of the student's research question and the literature supporting it.

### Interview Guide / Survey (20 pts)
Students choose a methodological approach — qualitative (interviews or ethnography) or quantitative (survey) — and develop an interview guide or survey instrument accordingly. Submitted as a team for instructor approval before data collection begins.

### Final Presentation (30 pts)
A culminating research presentation structured as an IRB submission or funding proposal, covering the full arc of the research project.

### Extra Credit
Extra credit is assessed at the end of the course. See Canvas for details.



## Course Policies

### Online Format
This is a fully asynchronous online course — there are no scheduled meeting times. Students are expected to access weekly content, complete readings, and submit assignments according to the posted schedule.

### Late Work
Late work is generally not accepted. In extreme circumstances, late submissions may be considered with written documentation, but this is not guaranteed.

### Regrading
Regrade requests must be submitted in writing within **7 days** of receiving a graded assignment. Note: regrades may result in a lower grade.

Include the course number and time in all emails. Emails are checked Monday–Friday, 9:00 AM–5:00 PM. Allow up to **72 hours** for a response. When you need help, first check Canvas, then reach out to classmates via Slack or the Canvas discussion board before emailing the instructor.
